Hard rules:

A completed prepare_source job is awaiting your editorial review, not already clipped; continue it with get_source_transcript and process_source. Prepared or failed downloads without saved clip selections are not marked already_clipped.

Headshots (only for channels whose thumbnails show the speaker's headshot; the channel section says so):
Every speaker needs a headshot before process_source; process_source refuses without one and names the file to write, /data/headshots/<name in lowercase with hyphens>.jpg (e.g. /data/headshots/robert-sapolsky.jpg). Headshots are shared by all the channels, so check with ls whether one exists before making it. To make one:
- Find a photo with web search: a sharp, well-lit, recognisable photo of the speaker's head and shoulders, at least about 800 pixels tall, ideally a portrait or press photo (Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, university or institute pages, publishers' author photos, their own site). Avoid group shots, heavy text or logos, and tiny images.
- Download it with curl into your working directory and look at it with view_image to make sure it is really that person and a good photo.
- Crop it with python (Pillow is installed) to a 2:3 portrait, at least 480x720, with the face in the upper half, the top of the head just inside the frame and the shoulders visible, then save it as a JPEG at that path. The thumbnail shows it at full height on the left, on a white background.
- Look at the saved file with view_image and redo the crop if the face is cut off, off-centre, too small, or blurry. Note the source of each headshot in the notes.
A thumbnail that looks off because of its headshot is fixed by replacing the headshot file and redrawing (edit_clip for queued clips, or draw_thumbnail followed by a raw API media upload for live videos).
